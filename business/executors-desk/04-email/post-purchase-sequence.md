### Email 1 — day 2
**Subject:** the clerk answers more than you'd think

The probate clerk's office cannot give legal advice. They will, however, tell you which form to file, the fee, whether your county does informal appointment, the local newspaper for creditor notice, and this year's small-estate limit. Most executors never call because they assume they need a lawyer to ask. Call. It is free, and it is the fastest way to fill in the deadline calendar on page 27.

If you have already been appointed, the four things to do today are on page 10: EIN, estate account, Form 56, post office.

### Email 2 — day 7

## B. Ledger pitch (buyers of the notebook who did not buy the Ledger, days 3, 5, 8)

### Email 1 — day 3
**Subject:** what months four to sixteen look like
Settling an estate takes about 16 months on average. After the first 90 days the job becomes bookkeeping: every dollar in and out of the estate account, categorized, reconciled, and eventually turned into an accounting the beneficiaries or court will accept. The Estate Ledger is the Google Sheet built for that, with the inventory, claims and distribution tabs linked to it. $37 for buyers of the notebook: [LINK]

### Email 2 — day 5
**Subject:** the receipts rule
No receipt, no reimbursement. Beneficiaries can challenge any expense you cannot document, and courts side with them. The Ledger turns every row amber until you tick the receipt box. It is a small thing and it is the thing that keeps executors out of arguments in month fourteen. [LINK]
