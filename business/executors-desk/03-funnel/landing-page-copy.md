### What this notebook is
Thirty-one pages, Letter size, designed to be printed and written on or filled in on a tablet. It is built around one idea: the first 90 days have a shape, and if you follow the shape you will not miss the things that cost executors money.

**Part One, the first 72 hours.** Ten items. Death certificates, the house, the will, the pets, the paperwork. With the reason beside each, because you will make better calls when you know why.

**Part Two, week one.** Securing and locating. The insurer call that keeps the house covered. The photographs that protect you from "where did Dad's watch go." The list of documents to find, and where people actually find them.

**Part Three, weeks two to four.** Whether you need probate at all (many estates do not), how to sort assets into the three piles that decide it, what "Letters" are and why nothing happens until you hold them, and the four things to do the day you get them.

**Part Four, days 30 to 90.** Creditors, the inventory, valuations, and the four tax returns an executor needs to know about, explained without a law degree.

### The tools (this is the part free checklists never include)
- **The "do not do this yet" list.** Twelve things people will pressure you to do in the first month, when you are actually allowed to do each, and what goes wrong if you do it early. Tape it to the fridge.
- **Seven phone scripts.** The bank before and after Letters. The credit card company. Social Security. Utilities. Life insurers. The homeowner's insurer. Read them out loud if you need to.
- **Three letters to copy.** Credit bureau deceased notice. Notice to a creditor with a date-of-death balance request. Date-of-death valuation request to a brokerage.
- **Trackers.** Death certificates (who has which copy). Call log. Mail log. Asset inventory. Debt inventory. Recurring bills. Digital accounts. Beneficiary contact sheet. Expense and mileage log. Weekly check-in.
- **The state quick reference.** Small-estate limits and creditor claim windows for the largest states as starting points, and exactly where to verify them for free.
- **The sibling page.** A monthly update email template that ends the "what is taking so long" texts, and five fair ways to divide personal things.
- **The lawyer page.** When you need one, when you do not, how they charge, and eight questions for the first call that will save you money on every call after.

### The five ways executors get personally burned
The notebook opens with these, because they are the point. Paying people too early. Mixing estate money with your own. Missing a deadline you did not know existed. Spending a benefit deposit that gets clawed back. Playing favorites. Each one is a page of plain language and a habit that prevents it.
