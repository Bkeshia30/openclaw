# Market research: picking the product

**Date:** September 2026
**Decision:** Sell a low-ticket PDF workbook to adult children who have just been named executor (or "personal representative") of a parent's estate in the United States.
**Working brand:** The Executor's Desk
**Core product:** *The First 90 Days: An Executor's Working Notebook* at $27

This document explains how the niche was chosen, what the numbers say, who the buyer is, who else is selling, and where the risks are. Sources are listed at the end.

---

## 2. The buyer

**Who:** An adult child, typically 45 to 65, who has just become the executor or personal representative of a parent's estate. Skews female. Often "the responsible sibling." Often out of state. Usually doing most of the work without a lawyer, or trying to keep lawyer hours low.

**What is happening to them right now:**
- They have a pile of mail, a house that is suddenly empty, and siblings texting them.
- They have been told they are "the executor" and do not know what that legally obligates them to do, or that they can be personally liable for mistakes (paying beneficiaries before creditors, missing tax deadlines, mixing estate money with their own).
- They are searching Google at night. Typical searches: "what to do when a parent dies checklist," "executor of estate duties," "how many death certificates do I need," "do I have to go through probate."

**Why they buy a $27 PDF instead of using a free checklist:**
- Free checklists from banks and insurers are one page, generic, and end with "call our advisors."
- They want *order*, not information: what to do this week, what not to do yet, and something to write in.
- They want the phone scripts and letter templates that free resources never include.
- $27 is cheaper than fifteen minutes of a probate attorney's time.

**Market size signals (US):**
- Roughly 2.6 million baby boomer deaths per year now, projected to reach 4 million per year by 2037. The leading edge of the boomer generation turned 80 in January 2026.
- Courts handle over 1.2 million probate cases a year. Oregon alone logged 13,736 probate filings in 2025, and about one in four deaths leads to a probate estate case.
- The average estate takes 16 months and about 570 hours of executor effort to settle (EstateExec national study).
- 46% of people named as executor did not know they had been chosen.
- Over 44% of executors experienced or witnessed family conflict during settlement.
- 55% of Americans die without a will, which means many "executors" are actually court-appointed administrators with even less guidance.

This is not a fad niche. It grows every year for the next twenty years for demographic reasons alone.

## 3. Demand signals

**Search intent** is the strongest signal. The phrases below are high-intent and informational, which is exactly what a $27 product converts from:
- what to do when a parent dies checklist
- executor checklist
- executor of estate duties / responsibilities
- how many death certificates do I need
- what happens if you don't probate a will
- small estate affidavit [state]
- notify credit bureaus of death
- can an executor be held personally liable
- estate account how to open

Google Ads CPC for estate-related legal terms sits at the low end of the legal category, which averages $8.67 across all practice areas. Informational executor terms are priced well below attorney-intent terms because most advertisers want litigation or estate-planning clients, not grieving families looking for a checklist.

**Marketplace:** Etsy shows a modest, active category ("executor planning binder," "executor checklist," "estate executor spreadsheet"). Listings sit between $5 and $25. One planner shows 72 favorites; a $9 checklist listed July 2026 had 6 favorites. This is a niche with buyers and few sellers, not a crowded one.

**Seasonality:** Deaths peak in January and February in the US. Estate planning searches also peak in January. Expect stronger conversion from December to March, with steady volume the rest of the year.

**Platform demographics:** 74% of Americans 50 to 64 use Facebook and 54% use it daily. Pinterest is used by about 50% of women versus 19% of men, which fits the buyer.

## 4. Competition

### Paid competitors
| Competitor | Format | Price | Weakness we exploit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Etsy "Executor Planning Binder" listings | Excel / PDF trackers | $5 to $25 | Trackers only. No sequencing, no scripts, no "don't do this yet" guidance. Design reads as generic planner. |
| SettleRight state workbooks (Etsy) | State-specific 15-page PDF | Under $20 (est.) | Openly AI-assisted content. Fifty separate listings dilute reviews. No voice. |
| Nolo, *The Executor's Guide* | 500-page book | About $30 to $40 | Excellent but overwhelming. Nobody reads 500 pages in the first week. |
| Amazon workbooks (Jackson, Meshram) | Print workbook | $15 to $25 | Ships in days. No templates, no scripts. |
| EstateExec | Software | $199 per estate | Expensive. Overkill for modest estates. |
| Alix, ClearEstate | Full service | Thousands | Different buyer. |

### Free competitors
Fidelity, Vanguard, AARP, TD, and every estate law firm publish a one-page checklist. They are lead magnets for advisors. They do not include letters, scripts, trackers, or sibling communication tools. Memorial Merits offers free worksheets and is the most direct free threat; it is content-heavy and ad-supported.

### Our position
"The first 90 days, in order, with the scripts and letters you actually need, written by someone who is not trying to sell you a lawyer."

Differentiators, in priority order:
1. **Sequence.** Week-by-week, with an explicit "do not do this yet" list.
2. **Tools.** Death-certificate tracker, call log, phone scripts, letter templates, house-vacancy checklist, sibling update template.
3. **Liability awareness.** The five mistakes that make executors personally liable, in plain language.
4. **Voice.** Written like a competent friend who has done this. No stock phrases.
5. **Format.** Fillable and printable. Letter size. Ink-light.

## 5. Pricing

| Item | Price | Role |
|---|---|---|
| The First 72 Hours checklist | Free | Lead magnet. Captures the email before the decision fatigue sets in. |
| The First 90 Days workbook | $27 | Core offer. Impulse range, still signals substance. |
| Scripts & Letters Pack | $9 order bump | Highest-margin add. Roughly 30 to 40% take rate is realistic for a bump this relevant. |
| Estate Ledger (Google Sheets: inventory, accounting, distribution) | $37 one-time offer | Post-purchase upsell for the months after the first 90 days. |
| Downsell: Ledger without video walkthrough | $19 | Shown only if the $37 is declined. |

Expected average order value with conservative take rates (35% bump, 15% upsell, 10% downsell): about $36.

Etsy price for the same workbook: $19.50. Etsy buyers are more price-sensitive and Etsy is a discovery channel, not the main funnel.

## 6. Unit economics (targets, not promises)

| Metric | Conservative | Expected |
|---|---|---|
| Landing page conversion (cold paid traffic to $27) | 1.5% | 3% |
| Opt-in rate for the free 72-hour checklist | 25% | 40% |
| Email conversion of opt-ins to buyers within 14 days | 5% | 10% |
| Average order value | $32 | $36 |
| Break-even cost per purchase (Meta or Google) | $32 | $36 |
| Google Search CPC on informational executor terms | $2.50 | $1.50 |
| Clicks needed per sale at 3% conversion | 33 | 33 |
| Implied cost per sale from Google Search | $83 (loses money) | $50 (loses money on first sale) |
| Implied cost per sale via opt-in path at 40% opt-in and 10% email conversion | $37 | $25 (profitable) |

Reading: direct-to-checkout from paid traffic will not be profitable at $27 in most weeks. The lead magnet path is what makes paid traffic work, because it converts over 14 days instead of one visit and the list has resale value for the $37 upsell. Organic Pinterest, SEO and Etsy have no traffic cost and are profitable from the first sale.

Meta benchmarks for 2026 put the median e-learning CPA at about $26.80 and cross-industry CPA at $38.19. Treat $30 as the cost-per-purchase ceiling for the tripwire and expect to break even on the front end while the upsell and list make the profit.

## 7. Platform decision

The brief asked for a GoHighLevel-style funnel "or whatever sells better." Here is the honest comparison for a single low-ticket product with full automation:

| Platform | Cost | Funnel + order bump + upsell | Email automation | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **systeme.io** | Free (2,000 contacts, 3 funnels, 1 automation rule), $17/mo Startup for 10 automations | Yes | Yes | **Start here.** Zero cost until the list grows. Upgrade to $17 when you add the second automation. |
| GoHighLevel Starter | $97/mo plus email/SMS usage | Yes | Yes, plus SMS | Use only if you already pay for it or plan to sell to other businesses. It is a $1,164 per year fixed cost against a $27 product. |
| ThriveCart | $495 one-time (+$295/yr Pro) | Best checkout on the market | No email (needs ConvertKit/MailerLite) | Good second-year upgrade once revenue justifies it. |
| Payhip | Free with 5% fee | Bump yes, upsell limited | Basic | Fine for a storefront, weak as a funnel. |
| Gumroad | 10% per sale | Limited | Basic | Most expensive per sale. Skip. |
| Stan Store | $29/mo | Basic | Basic | Built for Instagram/TikTok creators, not search buyers. Skip. |
| Etsy | $0.20 listing + 6.5% + 3% + $0.25 | No funnel | None (API blocks buyer emails) | **Run in parallel** as a discovery channel. Insert the free 72-hour checklist link inside the PDF to move Etsy buyers onto the list. |

Decision: systeme.io for the funnel, Etsy in parallel, a GoHighLevel mapping is provided in `03-funnel/gohighlevel-setup.md` for anyone who already has an account.

## 8. Traffic plan summary

Ranked by expected profitability for this niche:
1. **Google Search ads** on informational executor terms, sending to the free checklist page. Highest intent available anywhere.
2. **SEO articles** on the funnel domain answering the same questions. Slow to start, compounding, free.
3. **Pinterest** organic pins to both the free checklist and the Etsy listing. The audience is right and pins live for months.
4. **Etsy search** for the workbook and a $9 feeder product.
5. **Meta ads** with story-led static creatives, broad targeting age 45 to 65, sent to the free checklist. Meta removed "funeral planning" interest targeting and enforces its personal-attributes rule, so creative must never imply the reader has lost someone. Third-person story framing ("When my dad died, nobody told me...") passes; second-person ("You've just lost a parent") does not.
6. **Facebook groups and Reddit** (r/personalfinance, r/EstatePlanning) as slow, manual reputation channels. Optional.

Short-form video is deliberately deprioritized. The buyer is not on TikTok during a crisis, and the tone risk is high.

## 9. Risks and how they are handled

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Legal accuracy. State rules differ and change. | The workbook teaches federal and universal steps, gives state figures as "starting points," and tells the reader exactly where to verify (county probate court, state court self-help site). A "not legal advice" notice is on the cover and in the footer. A licensed-attorney review pass before launch is on the checklist and costs roughly $300 to $500. |
| Tone. Grief plus sales is easy to get wrong. | Voice guide in `08-brand/voice-guide.md`. No urgency countdown timers. No "limited spots." No exclamation marks. |
| Meta ad rejections. | Creative rules in `05-traffic/meta-ads.md`. Google Search is the primary paid channel anyway. |
| Free alternatives. | The product is a working tool, not information. Free checklists do not include scripts, letters, trackers or sequencing. |
| Chargebacks on grief purchases. | 30-day no-questions refund. Refund rate under 3% is normal for this format. |
| Looks AI-generated. | Specific scenes, specific numbers, first-person asides, deliberate imperfection in the prose, custom typography, no stock icons. See voice guide. |
