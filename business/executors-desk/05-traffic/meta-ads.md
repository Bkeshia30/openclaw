# Meta ads (Facebook and Instagram)

Second paid channel after Google Search. The audience is right (74% of Americans 50 to 64 use Facebook; 54% daily) but intent is lower and policy is stricter, so creative carries the whole load.

## Policy constraints that shape everything
- Meta's **personal attributes** rule: an ad may not assert or imply the reader's personal circumstances. "You've just lost a parent" fails. "When my dad died, nobody told me about the death certificates" passes (third person, the advertiser's story). Frame every ad as a story or a fact, never as a diagnosis of the viewer.
- Interest targeting such as "funeral planning" was removed. Target broad: US, ages 45 to 65, and let the creative and the pixel find the audience.
- No countdowns, no fake scarcity, no "limited copies."
- Landing page must have the privacy policy and the "not legal advice" line; reviewers check.

## Campaign structure (Advantage+ or manual, one campaign)
- Objective: Sales, optimizing for **Lead** (opt-in on `/72`) for the first 30 days, then switch to **Purchase** once the pixel has 50 purchases.
- Budget: $20 per day to start. Do not touch it for 7 days.
- Placements: Facebook feed, Instagram feed, Facebook Reels. Exclude Audience Network.
- One ad set, broad targeting, 6 to 10 creatives. Brands hitting top-quartile performance in 2026 test 6 to 10 concepts a month; that is the job here, not bid tweaking.

## Copy rules for this account
- Third person or first person, never "you have lost."
- One idea per ad. One link. No emoji.
- Comments: turn off if they become a grief wall you cannot moderate daily; otherwise reply once with the free link and nothing else.

## Kill and scale rules
- After 7 days: pause any creative with cost per opt-in over $6. Duplicate the best two with a new image.
- After 30 days: if cost per purchase (tracked through the email path with 14-day attribution) is under $30, raise budget 20% every 4 days. If over $40, stop Meta and put the budget into Google Search.
