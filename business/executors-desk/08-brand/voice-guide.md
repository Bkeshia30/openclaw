# Voice guide: how to not sound like a machine

The reader is 55, tired, grieving, and has been pitched by three law firms today. Every sentence has to earn the next one. This guide is the difference between "AI generic" and something a person keeps.

## The voice in one line
A competent friend who has done this before, sitting at the kitchen table with you, telling you what to do next and why.

## Rules
1. **Lead with the situation, not the product.** "There is a pile of mail on the kitchen table" beats "Are you overwhelmed by estate settlement?"
2. **Specific numbers, specific objects.** Ten to fifteen death certificates. 55°F. Page 8. The truck. The ring. Never "important documents"; say which ones.
3. **Give the reason.** Every instruction gets a "why." People follow instructions they understand and improvise on the ones they don't.
4. **Say what it is not.** Not legal advice. Not state-specific. Not long. Honesty about limits is the most persuasive thing on the page for this buyer.
5. **Short sentences. Verbs. One idea each.**
6. **No exclamation marks. No countdown timers. No "limited spots." No "unlock," "elevate," "journey," "navigate," "empower," "peace of mind," "seamless," "game-changer," "ultimate," "comprehensive."** If a phrase could appear on any product's page, delete it.
7. **No emoji. No icons next to bullets. No 3D ebook mockups.** Photograph the printed pages on a real desk.
8. **Admit friction.** "The bank will transfer you five times." "Executors burn out around week six." Readers trust writing that knows how it actually goes.
9. **Second person for instructions, first person for opinion.** "Order more than you think you need. I have never met an executor who ordered too many."
10. **Never fabricate a personal story.** If you have not settled an estate, do not write "when my dad died." Say who you built it with and what you checked. A small true reason beats a dramatic invented one, and this audience can smell the difference.
11. **Lowercase, plain subject lines.** "the phone is the key" not "🔑 The ONE Thing Every Executor Must Know!"
12. **End when the content ends.** No "I hope this helps!" No "Let me know if you have questions!" A signature is enough.

## Word swaps
| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| passed away | died |
| loved one | your parent, your mother, your father |
| navigate the process | do the steps in order |
| comprehensive guide | 30 pages, in order |
| peace of mind | you will not miss a deadline |
| leverage | use |
| utilize | use |
| in order to | to |
| it's important to note | (delete) |
| reach out | call, email, write |

## Before publishing anything, run the four tests
1. **The stranger test.** Could this sentence appear on a competitor's page unchanged? If yes, it is generic. Add the specific.
2. **The read-aloud test.** Read it out loud. Anywhere you would not say it to a person at a kitchen table, rewrite.
3. **The delete test.** Delete the first paragraph. Does the piece get better? Usually yes.
4. **The grief test.** Would this be fine to read the week after your own parent died? If it would make you wince, cut it.

## Design rules (same idea, visual)
- One typeface pair: Charter (or Georgia) for text, a plain sans (Liberation Sans, Helvetica, Inter) for headings and labels.
- One accent color: #2f4f4f. Black text. Off-white only for boxes.
- Whitespace over decoration. No stock photos of sunsets, hands, or candles.
- Real photographs of the printed pages, with a pen and a coffee ring if you like.
